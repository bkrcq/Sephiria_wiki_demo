## Sephiria build decision guide: choose a build with a method, not a guess

Most build advice tells you what to pick. This guide does something more useful: it walks you through how to decide. Work through the seven steps below with your own run notes, and you will end up with a build choice you can explain, test, and revise — instead of a recommendation you borrowed from someone whose run goal, skill level, and game version you do not know.

*Last updated: October 14, 2026. This is an original decision worksheet published by Sephiria Wiki. It uses only the confirmed game systems — weapons, artifacts, tablets, and animal synergies — and invents no item names, stats, or rankings.*

## Before you start: write down your run goal

A build is only "good" relative to a goal. Pick exactly one:

- **Learn** one weapon branch, room type, or mechanic.
- **Progress** through the current chapter.
- **Practice** one boss encounter repeatedly.
- **Cooperate** with a party of up to four players online.
- **Endure** the 60-level Hard Mode.

Write the goal at the top of your note. If you cannot pick one, you are planning two runs, not one. Split them.

## Step 1: Describe what you already have

Before choosing anything new, describe the last two or three runs in plain language:

- Which weapon branch did you use, and what range, speed, and damage style did it have?
- Which artifacts and tablets appeared, and what did each one observably do?
- Which animal synergies showed up, and alongside what combination?
- What ended each run, in one sentence each?

This step exists because most build decisions go wrong before they start: players choose a new build to fix a problem they never named. If your last three runs all ended the same way, that ending — not a tier list — is the problem your next build must solve.

## Step 2: Name the bottleneck

Every struggling run has a bottleneck: the one thing that ends runs before anything else gets a chance. Common bottlenecks, stated honestly:

- **Positioning.** You take avoidable damage because you stand in the wrong place at the wrong time.
- **Recovery.** You run out of ways to recover before the run ends.
- **Damage output.** Encounters drag on until a mistake becomes inevitable.
- **Range.** You cannot safely engage the encounters that end your runs.
- **Pace.** You spend too long per room or per floor and the run collapses under accumulated small errors.

Pick the bottleneck that actually ended your recent runs. If you pick the wrong bottleneck, the build you choose will fix a problem you do not have. When in doubt, re-read your run notes: the bottleneck is whatever appears in the "what ended the run" line most often.

## Step 3: List two or three candidate builds, not ten

Write down two or three candidate builds. Each candidate is a combination, not a single item:

- **Candidate A:** the weapon branch you already know best, with one change aimed at the bottleneck.
- **Candidate B:** a different weapon branch whose range, speed, and damage style address the bottleneck in a different way.
- **Candidate C (optional):** the same branch as A, but with a different play style — for example, more cautious positioning, or a different upgrade order.

Describe each candidate in one or two sentences: branch, intended fix for the bottleneck, and what you will observe. Do not write down ten candidates. Three is enough to decide; ten is enough to get lost.

## Step 4: Score each candidate on the four questions

Score each candidate from 1 to 3 on each question, using your own observed experience. These scores are your judgment, not game data — the point is to make your reasoning visible so you can check it later.

| Question | What a high score means |
| --- | --- |
| Can I execute it? | I can control this branch's range, speed, and timing consistently. |
| Does it fix the bottleneck? | It directly addresses what ended my recent runs. |
| Can I test it cleanly? | I can run it for three runs without changing five other things. |
| Does it fit the run goal? | It serves this run's single goal, not a different one. |

Add up the scores. The highest total is your leading candidate — not a proven winner, just the best-tested hypothesis. If two candidates tie, pick the one you can execute more consistently. Execution beats theory in every run this wiki has ever described.

## Step 5: Test the leader for three runs, changing one thing at a time

Run the leading candidate three times with the same goal, same mode, and same party size. Change exactly one thing per run if something needs adjusting — the weapon branch, one artifact choice, your positioning habit, or your upgrade order, but never all of them at once.

After each run, record:

- The weapon branch, artifacts, tablets, and animal synergies present.
- The mode, party size, chapter, and game version.
- What ended the run, in one sentence.
- Whether the bottleneck improved, stayed the same, or moved somewhere else.

If the bottleneck moved, you have learned something real: the build fixed the old problem and exposed the next one. That is progress, and it is exactly what the worksheet is for.

## Step 6: Decide with evidence, not memory

After three runs, compare the notes — not your memory of the best run. Ask:

- **Did the bottleneck improve in at least two of the three runs?** If yes, the candidate is working. Keep it and start fixing the next bottleneck.
- **Did the bottleneck stay the same?** The candidate did not address the real problem. Go back to Step 2 and re-name the bottleneck — your first diagnosis may have been wrong.
- **Did a new problem appear?** The candidate traded one bottleneck for another. Decide which bottleneck is easier to live with, or test Candidate B.
- **Was one run dramatically better than the others?** Treat it as an outlier until it repeats. A lucky run is not evidence.

Write the decision down in one sentence: "Candidate A fixes the recovery bottleneck in two of three runs, so it is my current progression build." A decision you can state in one sentence is a decision you can revisit honestly when new evidence arrives.

## Step 7: Schedule the re-check

No build decision is permanent. Re-check it when any of these happen:

- A patch lands. Balance changes can move the bottleneck.
- Your run goal changes. A progression build is not automatically a Hard Mode build.
- You switch between solo and co-op. Party play changes what a build must cover.
- Three consecutive runs end the same new way. A new pattern means a new bottleneck.

When you re-check, start again at Step 1 with fresh run notes. Do not defend an old decision against new evidence — the worksheet is cheaper than your time.

## Worked example (with placeholder labels, not game data)

This example uses placeholders on purpose. Replace them with your own observations; do not treat the labels as real item names.

> **Goal:** progress through the current chapter, solo.
> **Recent runs:** all three ended with the same cause — running out of recovery halfway through the chapter.
> **Bottleneck:** recovery, not damage.
> **Candidate A:** my familiar branch, but I spend the first upgrades on survival instead of damage, and I hold one upgrade back for flexibility.
> **Candidate B:** a longer-range branch I play less well, hoping distance reduces the damage I take.
> **Scores:** Candidate A scores higher on execution and testability; Candidate B scores higher on paper for avoiding damage. Total: A wins.
> **Test:** three runs with Candidate A. Runs 1 and 3 reach further than any previous run; Run 2 ends early to a positioning mistake.
> **Decision:** Candidate A is my current progression build. Next bottleneck to fix: positioning.

Notice what the example does not do: it does not claim a branch is best, it does not cite damage numbers, and it does not promise a result. It shows the reasoning chain a reader can copy with their own data.

## Co-op: decide the party, then the builds

In co-op, apply the worksheet to the party first. Online co-op supports up to four players, and the developer's 1.0 announcement describes teammates trading items and reviving fallen companions. That means the party can split responsibilities: one player can prioritize recovery while others prioritize damage or range, instead of everyone building the same way.

Before a co-op session, agree on:

- Each player's run goal for the session.
- Who covers which range, and who carries recovery.
- How you will record the session: chapter, mode, party size, game version, and what ended the run.

After the session, score the party's plan the same way you would score a solo build. A party plan that survives three sessions is worth more than any individual build inside it. See the [co-op guide](/coop) for the confirmed scope and the details that still need a primary source.

## Hard Mode: decide for endurance

Hard Mode contains 60 levels, so the worksheet's bottleneck question matters more than ever. Damage-heavy plans that look strong on the first floors often run out of recovery later. When choosing a Hard Mode build, score candidates on endurance: can this plan survive the floors where your runs actually end? Record which floor each run reached and what ended it. Over several runs, the pattern will tell you whether the bottleneck is damage, survival, or a mistake you keep repeating — and the worksheet will tell you what to change next.

## Mistakes this worksheet prevents

- **Choosing by reputation.** A build recommended without a run goal, a game version, or a test method is an opinion, not a plan.
- **Fixing the wrong bottleneck.** Damage upgrades cannot fix a recovery problem, and survival upgrades cannot fix a pace problem.
- **Changing everything at once.** Five simultaneous changes produce a result you cannot learn from.
- **Remembering only the best run.** Notes beat memory. Compare all three test runs, not the one that felt great.
- **Treating a decision as permanent.** Patches, goal changes, and new evidence all justify a re-check.

## Frequently asked questions

### What is the best Sephiria build?

No universal best build is confirmed. Use the worksheet: name your run goal, diagnose the bottleneck from your own run notes, test one candidate for three runs, and decide from the evidence.

### How do I choose between two builds?

List both as candidates, score them on execution, bottleneck fit, testability, and goal fit, then test the leader for three runs with one variable changed at a time.

### How many runs should I test a build?

Three runs with the same goal is a reasonable minimum. Fewer runs can suggest a lead but do not establish reliability.

### Should my co-op build differ from my solo build?

Usually, yes. In co-op, the party can split ranges and roles, and teammates can trade items and revive fallen companions according to the developer's 1.0 announcement. Plan the party first, then the individual builds.

### What if my bottleneck diagnosis was wrong?

The three-run test will show it: the bottleneck will stay the same while the build changes. Go back to Step 2, re-read your run notes, and re-name the bottleneck.

### When should I re-check a build decision?

After a patch, after a goal change, after switching between solo and co-op, or after three consecutive runs end the same new way.
